Introductory Challenge 3: Rethinking Movement

Hi there.

Thanks for taking a few minutes for yourself today.

Let’s talk about seeing exercise and physical activity in a whole new way—one that’s not just about knowing it’s good for your health, but about actually enjoying it and making it easier to stick with. And the best part? This isn’t just a nice idea—it’s backed by science!

We often think of physical activity or exercise as something we should do… something hard, time-consuming, or uncomfortable. For many people—especially those who don’t move much—exercise can feel painful, inconvenient, or like something they have to do just to lose weight, which might be your goal. Even if they start out motivated, those feelings often make them stop.

But what if movement could feel different?

Step 1: Move in Ways That Feel Good to You.

Let’s begin with a simple idea: Move in ways that feel good.

  • Ask yourself: What kind of movement actually feels good to me?
  • It could be dancing in your kitchen, stretching in bed, or walking outdoors with a friend.

You don’t have to follow a strict workout plan to benefit. You just need to find what feels right for you. New research shows that how we feel during physical activity affects whether we’ll want to do it again. When movement feels good, your brain remembers that—and it increases your chances of repeating it.

You might try choosing activities not just to cross them off a to-do list, but because they actually feel good while you’re doing them. Instead of thinking, “I have to take a walk,” you could start thinking of it as something you get to do—something to enjoy.

That’s the shift: turning exercise from a “have to” into a “want to”—a gift you give yourself.

Step 2: Everything Counts. It All Adds Up.

Here’s something many people don’t realize: Every bit of activity counts—no matter how small.

You don’t need to do a long workout at the gym to make progress.

  • Walking the dog, doing chores, or even standing up to stretch—it all adds up.
  • Just 2 minutes of movement is worth doing.

For a long time, we’ve been told that physical activity only counts if it’s long, intense, or done “right.” But that kind of thinking can be discouraging. If it’s not perfect, we often feel like it’s not worth doing at all.

But here’s a better way to think about it:

Let’s say you usually skip the gym unless you have a full hour. But now, you start thinking, “Maybe I don’t need a full hour—maybe a 15-minute walk is still worth it.” So instead of skipping it, you go anyway. You walk a lap or two. You stretch. You move a little. And you feel good for doing something.

That’s the mindset: Shorter activity still matters. It’s easier to fit into your day, and it helps build confidence and momentum. You might walk a little more. Take an extra trip upstairs. Do a stretch break at work. These micro-movements add up—and they matter.

And when you combine “everything counts” with doing what feels good, you begin to reframe the meaning of physical activity—not as something to check off a list, but as something that supports your life.

Step 3: Give Yourself Permission to Prioritize Self-Care.

This one might be the hardest—but maybe the most important.

So many people feel guilty taking time for themselves. But here’s the truth: making time for exercise or activity is a smart and necessary form of self-care. When you move, you support your mental health, your energy, and your ability to do the things that matter most.

Giving yourself permission to move for you can break down some of the biggest barriers—like feeling too busy or thinking self-care is selfish. It helps you see movement as a way to fuel your day, your focus, and your priorities—not as something extra or optional.

You’re not being selfish by choosing to move. You’re being strong. You deserve to feel better and have more energy for the people and things that matter to you.

So go ahead—give yourself permission to take care of yourself.

Let’s Review

Step 1: Move in ways that feel good to you

Step 2: Remember that everything counts

Step 3: Give yourself permission to prioritize your well-being

You don’t have to do it all at once. Just take one small step today. One choice to move. One kind decision for yourself.

You’ve got this.
